## 摘要
人体运动由一个分层运动系统控制，其中大脑提供高级意图，较低层级的结构协调详细的动力学。现有的脑机接口（BCI）通常将其过度简化为受限的分类或低维控制，无法捕获自然运动的丰富性。由于稀疏神经信号与高维运动学之间存在显著的跨模态差异，以及缺乏大规模配对的EEG-运动数据集，弥合这一差距以实现开放词汇、全身运动合成仍然具有挑战性。为此，我们引入了EEG2MOTION，这是首个用于人体运动合成的EEG-运动-文本数据集，包含近20,000个配对样本，涵盖数千种运动。利用该数据集，我们首先通过多模态对比学习证明，非侵入性EEG嵌入可以有效地与文本、视频和运动表示对齐，以解码高级语义。然后，我们提出了EEG条件掩码运动模型（EMMM），这是一个生成框架，将EEG编码器与运动解码器相结合，直接从大脑活动合成连续的全身人体运动。实验结果表明，EMMM能够从非侵入性脑信号生成连贯且逼真的运动序列。据我们所知，这是首次从非侵入性脑信号生成多样化全身人体运动的工作，为生成式和开放词汇的运动脑机接口开辟了新方向。参见我们的项目页面：https://yulom.github.io/EEG2MOTIONdemopage/。

## Abstract
Human motion is governed by a hierarchical motor system where the brain provides high-level intentions and lower-level structures coordinate detailed dynamics. Existing brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) typically oversimplify this into constrained classification or low-dimensional control, failing to capture the richness of natural movement. Bridging this gap to achieve open-vocabulary, full-body motion synthesis remains challenging due to the substantial cross-modal divergence between sparse neural signals and high-dimensional kinematics, as well as the lack of large-scale paired EEG-motion datasets. To address this, we introduce EEG2MOTION, the first EEG-motion-text dataset for human motion synthesis, comprising nearly 20,000 paired samples across thousands of motions. Using this dataset, we first demonstrate via multimodal contrastive learning that non-invasive EEG embeddings can be effectively aligned with text, video, and motion representations to decode high-level semantics. We then propose EEG-conditioned Masked Motion Model (EMMM), a generative framework that unites an EEG encoder with a motion decoder to synthesize continuous, full-body human motions directly from brain activity. Experimental results show that EMMM generates coherent and realistic motion sequences from non-invasive brain signals. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to generate diverse full-body human motions from non-invasive brain signals, opening a new direction toward generative and open-vocabulary motor BCIs.
